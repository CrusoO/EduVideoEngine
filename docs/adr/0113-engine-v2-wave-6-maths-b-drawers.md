# ADR 0113: Engine V2 wave 6 (Maths B: statistics, probability, calculus, sequences, matrices, complex numbers, conics, linear programming, diagrams) and the minus sign in a label

Status: Proposed, 2026-11-12. The owner accepts it. Builds on ADR 0100, 0107, 0108, 0110, 0111 and 0112. Touches no frozen file and no item of section 15 of the architecture. `docs/ENGINE_INTERFACE.md` is unchanged.

## Decision, part 1 (committed first): a leading minus sign in a label
The closed label alphabet of an object (`SAFE_LABEL` in `packages/pipeline/objects/spec.ts`, wave 0) began with "a letter or a digit", so a label could not start with a minus sign: the label of a negative number on a number line or an axis had to be written `x=-2`. Wave 5 worked round it and listed it as a limit (ADR 0112, "Limits of what is drawn").
1. A label may now start with a minus sign, the hyphen-minus or the Unicode minus (U+2212), **only when a digit follows it at once** (`-2`, `-3.5`, and the same with U+2212). A minus before a letter, two signs, a lone sign and a sign followed by a space are still refused. The Unicode minus is also allowed inside a label (`x=` then U+2212 then `2`). The total stays 1 to 60 characters, the sign counted.
2. Nothing the old alphabet allowed is refused, and nothing it refused for another reason is let in: a seeded property test compares the old and the new pattern on 4000 random strings that do not start with a minus. A label is still refused if it holds personal data, a secret or a banned positioning phrase.
3. The story sanitiser keeps such a text unchanged (its list-marker rule needs a space after the dash, which the alphabet does not allow after a leading minus), and a test shows a drawn text with the Unicode minus reaches the page. With `ENGINE_OBJECTS_V2` off the page bytes are identical (test Q1 in `objects-label-minus.test.ts`): the alphabet is read only when an object request is parsed or a drawing is laid out.
4. The older drawers are untouched: their tick labels keep the form they have (`x=-2`), and the older tests that hold a pattern of their own for the old alphabet still pass because the new pattern accepts everything the old one did. The wave-6 drawers use the leading hyphen-minus for the tick labels of an axis (the Argand diagram); a frame in real Chromium shows it drawn in the engine's font. The Unicode minus is allowed by the alphabet but no drawer uses it yet.

## Context
ADR 0112 built the geometry and graphs half of the maths table and left the other half to this wave (its "What this wave does not do"). The owner asked on 2026-11-12 for the rest of the table of `OBJECT-COVERAGE-8-12.md`: statistics (histogram, frequency polygon, ogive, box plot, scatter with regression, mean, median and mode figures, normal and binomial distributions), probability (trees, Venn diagrams, sample spaces, dice and coin tables, conditional probability), calculus (the derivative as the slope of a tangent, the area under a curve as Riemann sums, optimisation, limit graphs), sequences and series, matrices and determinants, the Argand diagram, conics, linear programming, inverse trigonometric graphs, flowcharts and mapping diagrams, two-column proofs, and the objects of wave 5's plan that were still open (square and cube models, fraction bars, the algebraic identity as an area model, the real-number tree and the square-root spiral). The method is that of waves 1 to 5.

## Decision, part 2: the drawers
1. **Which drawers.** 25 drawers, one per figure, in `packages/pipeline/objects/maths/` (all of the `maths` request kind, so `MATHS_FIGURES` lists 47 figures):
   - statistics, `stats.ts`: `m_freq` (histogram, frequency polygon, less-than ogive with the median read across), `m_centre` (a dot plot with the mean line and the median line, or a box plot with its whiskers), `m_scatter` (points, the least-squares line, the mean point), `m_normal` (the curve to four standard deviations, a shaded area between two limits or the 1, 2 or 3 standard deviation bands), `m_binom` (the exact bars with the event shaded, or a seeded simulation of up to 400 runs with the exact probabilities laid over the bars);
   - probability, `chance.ts`: `m_ptree` (two draws from a bag with or without replacement, in exact fractions, or branch probabilities given and checked), `m_venn` (two sets with the count of every region, and P(A given B)), `m_sample` (the grid of two dice, two coins, three coins, a coin and a die, with an event shaded);
   - calculus, `calculus.ts`: `m_tangent` (the tangent, or a secant whose slope approaches it, for x squared, x cubed, square root, sine, e to the x and ln x), `m_riemann` (left, right or middle rectangles and the exact area), `m_optim` (the fence and the open box), `m_limit` (a hole, a jump, a value approached at infinity);
   - sequences, matrices and complex numbers, `seqmat.ts`: `m_seq` (AP terms, AP partial sums, GP terms, a convergent GP with its sum to infinity), `m_matrix` (the unit square and its image under a 2 by 2 matrix with the determinant, or two equations as two lines solved by Cramer's rule), `m_argand` (a number and its conjugate, a sum, a product, the roots of unity);
   - `curves.ts`: `m_conic` (parabola in four directions, ellipse, hyperbola, each with foci, one directrix and a point P), `m_lp` (up to three constraints with x and y at least 0, the feasible region, the corner points, the objective line and the best corner; or the region alone, which is also the shading of an inequality in the plane), `m_invtrig` (arcsin, arccos, arctan with the restricted function and the mirror line y = x);
   - `diagrams.ts`: `m_flow` (even or odd, the larger of two, the sum 1 to n by a loop, with the path taken for the input in colour), `m_mapping` (a rule or arrows given one by one), `m_proof` (six two-column proofs);
   - `models.ts`: `m_blocks` (the grid of a square with its odd-number last layer, a cube as three faces of unit cells, and the whole-number root of a perfect square or cube), `m_fracbar` (a fraction bar, two bars to compare, a ratio bar), `m_identity` ((a+b)^2, (a-b)^2, (a+b)(a-b) and (x+p)(x+q) as areas), `m_realtree` (the sets a number belongs to, and the square-root spiral).
2. **Own geometry only, no data.** As in wave 5: pure TypeScript, licence `owner-original`, source `own-drawings`, no tool, no `toolVersions`, no new dependency, the source register unchanged. The only data are conventions: quartiles by the median of each half (the middle value left out of both halves when the count is odd), the population standard deviation, the principal argument in (-180, 180], the seeded generator of the simulation (a 32-bit linear congruential generator, so the same seed gives the same run on every machine).
3. **Requests.** `params` are numbers, booleans and closed lists, strict per figure (`bparams.ts`); a data set is a fixed list of numbered fields (`d1` to `d10`, `x1`, `y1` to `x7`, `y7`, `f1` to `f6`), never a string of numbers. A figure takes the whole stage grid, as in wave 5.
4. **Impossible inputs are refused with the rule they break.** A probability outside 0 to 1; branches from one point that do not add up to 1; p of 0 or 1 for a binomial (a certain result has no distribution); k above n; a Venn diagram whose overlap is bigger than a set or whose sets are bigger than the universe; P(A given B) with B empty; an event that makes no sense for the sample space (the sum of two coins, a sum of 13, an event that is the whole space); a standard deviation of 0 or below; limits for the shading beyond four standard deviations; a lower limit above the upper; an ogive with an empty class; a box plot of equal values or with equal quartiles; a scatter whose x are all equal (no regression of y on x) or whose y are all equal (r undefined); a tangent where there is no slope (ln of a negative number, the square root at 0); a secant with h of 0; an area that dips below the axis; a limit said to exist when the two sides differ, or not to exist when they agree, or a hole said to stop a limit; a GP said to converge with |r| of 1 or more, a ratio of 0 or 1, terms that grow past 5000, an AP with difference 0; a matrix said to have an inverse when its determinant is 0 (and the reverse); a pair of equations said to have one solution when the lines are parallel or the same line (and the reverse); z = 0 where an argument is asked; two complex numbers that cancel; an ellipse with a = b (a circle: the foci meet); a parabola with a focal length that is not positive; an empty feasible region, a maximum or minimum over a region that is unbounded in that direction, an objective with both coefficients 0; arcsin or arccos of a number outside -1 to 1; a mapping with an element that has two images said to be a function (and the reverse), a many-one function said to be one-one, an into function said to be onto; a fraction above one whole in a bar; a comparison of fractions that is not the one said; (a + b)^2 said to be a squared plus b squared, (a - b)^2 said to be a squared minus b squared; a number that is not a perfect square or cube said to be one; a square root said to be rational or irrational when it is not; a converse of Pythagoras on sides that do not satisfy it. The router turns any refusal into a note and no drawing.
5. **Truth from computed numbers.** Every number comes from a tested pure function in `bcompute.ts` and is printed once as `key = value unit` to three significant figures, or as an exact fraction where the figure works in fractions (a tree, a Venn diagram, a sample space, a fraction bar). `tests/objects-maths-b-compute.test.ts` checks them: the grouped mean against the mean of the expanded data, the median read across the ogive against the interpolated median (200 random class sets), the quartiles and modes against known values, the regression against a perfect line, the symmetry of r and the least squares (two nearby lines are worse, 200 random sets), `erf` against known values and the density against its distribution function by Simpson's rule (30 random normals), the binomial against Pascal's rule, a sum of 1, the mean np and the variance npq (100 random cases), the seeded simulation (repeatable, counts every trial, close to the exact bars for 20000 runs), the tree against exact fractions (200 random bags, leaves add to 1, an event and its complement), every event of two dice against counting, Venn regions (300 random sets), each derivative against a central difference and each antiderivative against Simpson (all six functions), the Riemann sums (left and right bracket, middle is closer, all converge), the optimisation against a scan of 2000 points (60 random sizes, both problems), AP and GP against a running loop and a 400-term partial sum, the determinant against the shoelace area of the image and the inverse against the matrix (300 random matrices), the solution of two equations against a built-in solution (300 cases), the product of complex numbers (moduli multiply, arguments add, 200 cases), the roots of unity, each conic against its defining property at random points (parabola in every direction, 100 ellipses, 100 hyperbolas), linear programming against a brute-force search of a grid (150 random programmes, with empty and unbounded 6. **The existing verifier, where it applies.** `bclaims.ts` states the facts of 22 of the 25 figures that the closed expression language (`num sym neg add sub mul div pow eq`) can state, as in wave 5: the grouped mean and the interpolated median; the mean, median, quartiles and range of raw data; the regression slope and intercept; the probabilities of a binomial adding to 1 and the mean np; the leaves of a tree adding to 1 and the probability of an event; inclusion and exclusion, the four regions of a Venn diagram, P(A given B); the slope of the tangent and of a secant of x squared and x cubed; a Riemann sum and the exact area of x squared and x cubed; the zero of the slope in the fence and the box problems and their greatest values; the limit of a hole and the values of f(10) and f(100); the nth term and the sum of an AP and a GP, the sum to infinity and what is still to add; a determinant, an inverse, the solution of two equations by Cramer's rule and its substitution into both; the modulus squared, the sum and the product of complex numbers; the focal distance and the latus rectum of a conic; the best corner of a linear programme; the sum 1 to n; the image of an element under a rule; the arithmetic of a proof; a fraction in lowest terms; an identity of areas; the perfect root of a number. The test runs all of them in one SymPy process (the 173 sample figures, more than 200 equalities, all verified), shows that making one stated number wrong by one fails every one of those figures (the copy shares no nodes, so a number cannot be changed in one place and stay right in another), and runs 200 random AP and GP, bag trees and matrices. A first draft found a fraction by a search up to a denominator of 10000 with a loose tolerance, and the verifier rightly refused a sum whose value had been rounded to a wrong neighbour: `rational` now finds the fraction by continued fraction up to a denominator of a million and accepts it only to 12 figures. **Not in the verifier's language, so not verified by it:** the normal curve and its areas (the exponential and the error function), the correlation coefficient r (a square root), sine, e to the x and ln x in the tangent, secant and area figures, the roots of unity and any distance with a square root (the conics' distances, the modulus of a complex number), the inverse trigonometric functions, the sample-space counts (checked by enumeration and read back, with no equation to prove), the square root of a number that is not a perfect square, and every inequality (the shading of a region and the direction of a ray). ## Data of record used
None. Conventions used, stated in `bcompute.ts`: angles in degrees at the edges and in radians inside; y up on a graph; quartiles by the median of each half; population standard deviation; the principal argument of a complex number in (-180, 180]; a bag's balls are red (R) and blue (B), a tree with given probabilities names its outcomes A and B; the unit-square corners (0,0), (1,0), (1,1), (0,1); the parabola y^2 = 4ax turned to the direction asked, the ellipse and hyperbola centred at the origin with the foci on the x axis (the y axis for an ellipse with b above a); the square-root spiral starts with a leg of 1 from the origin and each new short side is 1 at a right angle to the radius.

## What this wave does not do
- **Not drawn:** a pie chart; a bar chart of categories (the histogram has equal class widths, and unequal widths with frequency density are not drawn); a counting tree, arrangements and selections; Pascal's triangle and the binomial expansion; a slope field and solution curves; the derivative graph (the graph of f' beside f) and the area between two curves; a trapezoid or Simpson rule; row operations, a 3 by 3 matrix, the composition of matrix transformations; a polar form and de Moivre; a conic not in standard position, a cone slice, the second directrix; integer programming; the three-set Venn diagram, a tree of more than two stages or more than two outcomes, a sample space for the sum of three dice; a truth table and a Cartesian product; place-value charts, equation steps, the balance scale, the factor tree, and the placement of an irrational number on the number line (the real-number tree says which sets it belongs to, and the spiral builds the length); a flowchart of any algorithm other than the three (a general layout engine was not built); animations of any kind (the secant approaching the tangent is a figure per value of h, not a motion).
- **Limits of what is drawn:** one figure per scene on the whole grid, at most 24 shapes (the flowchart, the Argand diagram with eight roots and the binomial simulation sit at the limit). Data sets are small by design (up to 6 classes, 10 values, 7 points, a binomial of up to 12 trials, 8 terms, 4 domain elements). A Venn diagram is schematic: the circles are the same size whatever the counts. The terminators of a flowchart are rectangles, and the loop-back arrow is one straight arrow. A cube is three faces, not a solid with hidden edges. The normal curve stops at four standard deviations. A tangent or secant is clipped to the window. An ellipse with a very long axis is drawn small, as the window is square. The fraction bar holds at most 12 parts.
- **Not verified:** see item 6. In addition: the labels (letters, names of the sets, the words of a proof) are checked for being the words the closed lists give, not for being the right idea; the plan's chapter lists remain `UNVERIFIED` against the official syllabus; the text of a proof is checked against a closed library of six theorems, not against a general proof checker. The frames were looked at in real Chromium: 40 sample frames and 4 of the 6 stories (20 frames) were drawn and read back, and about a dozen of them were looked at closely (the Argand product, the tree, the hyperbola and parabola, linear programming, two flowcharts, a Venn diagram, an identity, an ogive, Riemann rectangles). The first look found a tree label struck through by its branch, the focus labels sitting on their dots, text sunk to the foot of its box in the flowchart and a label on top of an arrow, all changed; the other frames were not looked at. No live model call, network or paid service was used.
- **Not reviewed:** no maths teacher has looked at any figure. The reviewer is `[owner to name]`. Every drawer is `reviewed: false`.
