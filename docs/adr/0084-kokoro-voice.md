# ADR 0084: Kokoro as a second voice, beside Piper's development voice

Status: proposed (agent, at the owner's request of 2026-10-23: "wire it in as a second voice with an ADR, and make the Ohm's law video with it so I can compare"; the owner approved installing `kokoro-onnx` and `soundfile` and downloading about 205 MB of model files in chat; the owner is asked to confirm this record). Supersedes nothing; the voice stays `development-only`. No bundle, sentence track hash rule or manifest field changes.

## Context
The only voice was Piper's Jenny (Dioco): it sounds mechanical and it cannot ship, because the model was fine-tuned from a voice trained under a research-only licence (ADR 0013, ARCHITECTURE section 8). The owner's product has a free and a paid tier, so a voice whose weights and training data are clear matters. A search of the Kokoro-82M model card and its ONNX conversions (reported by the authors and by third-party pages, not verified here) found: weights under Apache 2.0; English training audio that is public domain, permissively licensed or generated by closed commercial TTS services; two CC BY sets (Japanese, French) in v1.0 that English does not use; about 54 voices; CPU use through ONNX.

## Decision
1. **`kokoroSynthesizer(tools, { voice, speed, lang })`** (`packages/pipeline/build/kokoro.ts`) implements the same `Synthesizer` interface as Piper. A Python worker (`scripts/voice/kokoro_worker.py`, run in isolated mode) is started once and voices every sentence of a build; the model loads once. The adapter queues requests (the worker voices one sentence at a time), reports a sentence that fails alone, with its reason, and a worker that cannot start, with what it printed. `dispose()` ends it; the build tool calls it.
2. **The track hash is unchanged** (words, voice id, model version). The voice id is `kokoro:<voice>` (default `bf_emma`, British female); the model version holds the package version, the first 12 characters of the model and voices file hashes, the speed and the first 8 characters of the worker's hash, so a change of file, pace or leveling is another track and no file made at another setting is ever reused.
3. **Pace and level.** Default speed 0.92 (the voice at 1.0 read 192 words a minute against Jenny's 177; at 0.92 it reads 185 on the Ohm lesson). The worker brings every sentence to the same level (RMS -15 dBFS, peak at most -0.5 dBFS), because the voice came out 6 dB quieter than Jenny and uneven between sentences. Measured on the Ohm's law video: mean volume -17.8 dB (Jenny -14.5), peak -0.4 dB.
4. **Files**, in `tools/kokoro/` (not committed): `kokoro-v1.0.fp16.onnx` (177 MB) and `voices-v1.0.bin` (28 MB) from the kokoro-onnx release `model-files-v1.0`; Python packages `kokoro-onnx` (MIT), `onnxruntime` (MIT) and `soundfile` (BSD-3-Clause).
5. **Choosing it**: `pnpm lesson:build <skill> --voice kokoro [--kokoro-voice af_heart]`; Piper stays the default. Voice files go to the lesson's `voice/` folder; to keep both voices of one lesson use `--out` for one of them (the comparison used `dist/lessons-kokoro`).
6. **Status is `development-only`.** The manifest says so, the build guard refuses it in production, and the credit line "AI-generated voice: Kokoro" is shown wherever it is heard. It becomes `cleared` only when the owner has read the licence files of the model and of its training-data statement and a listening test with 3 to 5 teachers (ARCHITECTURE section 8) prefers it. This ADR does not decide that.

7. **The default of `pnpm lesson:make` is Kokoro `bf_emma`** (the owner preferred it to Jenny after listening, 2026-10-23), and Piper when the Kokoro files are not installed; `--voice piper` or `--voice kokoro` says which. `pnpm lesson:build` keeps Piper as its default. The tool prints the voice, its status and that it is labelled as AI-generated.

## What is left before the voice can be `cleared`
Read in the installed packages and the model card on 2026-10-23; none of it is legal advice.
1. **The model card's training-data statement is the authors' word.** It says: Apache 2.0 weights; training audio that is public domain, permissively licensed, or synthetic audio from closed commercial TTS services (none from open models or voice clones); two CC BY sets (Koniwa `tnc`, under an hour, CC BY 3.0; SIWIS, under eleven hours, CC BY 4.0) used for v1.0. Open questions for a lawyer: whether the CC BY credit of those two sets must travel with the voice (our English voices do not use those languages), and whether the "closed commercial TTS" origin of some training audio is compatible with those providers' terms. Nothing here can be checked from the weights.
2. **`phonemizer` 3.4.0 (GPL-3.0-or-later) and `espeak-ng` (GPL-3.0, loaded by `espeakng-loader`) are installed by `kokoro-onnx`.** They turn text into sounds before the model runs. Used as a build tool, with only the audio leaving this machine, their licences do not bind the audio. They would bind the engine if it ever *shipped* a build with them (an app, an installer, a server image given to others). Decision for the owner and counsel: keep Kokoro strictly on the build machine, or replace the phonemiser (`misaki`, which is Apache but falls back to espeak-ng for unknown words).
3. **A copy of each licence is kept on file** (`config/sources.json`-style evidence, ADR 0078): the model card at the commit used, the `LICENSE` of `kokoro-onnx` (MIT), `onnxruntime` (MIT), `soundfile` (BSD-3-Clause), `numpy`, `phonemizer` (GPL-3.0) and the release page of the two model files, with their SHA-256 hashes (they are in each manifest).
4. **A blind listening test** with 3 to 5 teachers (ARCHITECTURE section 8), on the same lessons, Kokoro against Piper's Jenny and against the cloud candidate if one is tried, including a maths read-aloud check (every number, every equation read correctly) and a pace check with learners.
5. **The owner's decision** that this voice is the production voice, recorded here by an edit of this ADR, and then `status: "cleared"` set in `kokoroSynthesizer`. Until then the build guard refuses it in production.
6. **Accent and language fit** for Bhutan and India: American and British English only. If learners need an Indian or Bhutanese accent, or Hindi, Dzongkha or Nepali, this voice is not enough (see below).
7. **Attribution in the product**: the credit line "AI-generated voice: Kokoro" is shown wherever it is heard; the licence notice of an Apache 2.0 work goes in the product's notices page.

## Measured (4-core laptop, no GPU, fp16 model)
20 sentences voiced in about 1 min 40 s through one worker (about 5 s a sentence, with an 8 s model load), against 21 s for 25 sentences with Piper and two jobs. fp16 weights are slow on a CPU without fp16 arithmetic; the full-precision file (325 MB) or the int8 file (92 MB; measurably lower quality in its own notes) may be faster and are not tried here. The voice cache means a sentence is voiced once per voice and setting.

## Not built
- Indian-English or Bhutanese-accent voices, Hindi, Dzongkha and Nepali: Kokoro has American and British English and only partial Hindi. Indic Parler-TTS is the candidate for those (gated; licence shown as Apache 2.0 on Hugging Face and MIT on India's AIKosh, to be checked in the licence file; much slower on a CPU).
- Several workers in parallel, a faster model file, word-level timing from the voice.
- The listening test, and the clearing of the voice.
