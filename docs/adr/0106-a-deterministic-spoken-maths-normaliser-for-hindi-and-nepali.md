# ADR 0106: A deterministic spoken-maths normaliser for Hindi and Nepali

Status: Proposed, 2026-11-11. The owner accepts it. Extends ADR 0006 (the spoken form is always words from a verbaliser), ADR 0096 and 0097 (pause markers, one place for speech preparation). Supersedes nothing. Every Hindi wording is an engineer's proposal and every Nepali wording is unchecked: nothing here is released until a native speaker has reviewed it.

## Context
The owner's research (2026-11-11): Hindi text-to-speech is good enough for a pilot with the paid vendors (Sarvam Bulbul v3, ElevenLabs v3); the Kokoro Hindi voices are graded C; Nepali has no published quality score, so Nepali starts with a native narrator while TTS is bake-off tested. Scripts are written natively, not translated, in the आप register for Hindi and तपाईं for Nepali.
Before any TTS, every maths symbol has to be words, because a vendor's own text normaliser is not to be trusted with an equation. Today `speechText` (ADR 0097) writes a handful of signs as English words and leaves digits, fractions, units and formulas to the voice, and the schema's `verbalise` (ADR 0006) refuses Hindi and Nepali until a native reviewer approves a vocabulary. This ADR is the engine-side piece that lets a reviewer approve one: the words are data, the rules are code, and both are testable without audio.

## Decision
1. **One pure function.** `spokenMath(text, { language: "hi" | "ne", register: "hinglish" | "ncert" })` in `packages/pipeline/build/spoken-math/`. `spokenMathDetailed` also returns the lexicon entries used, the Nepali entries with no wording yet, the number of equations and the pause. No model, network, clock or randomness.
2. **What it expands.** Whole numbers (Indian grouping up to 99 crore; zero-led or very long strings digit by digit), decimals, fractions (3/4, ¾), mixed numbers, signs and operators, powers (`x^2`, `x²`, `(a+b)^3`, `10^-3`), square and cube roots, percent, ranges, ordinals (1st), years and dates, units (km, m/s, m/s², °C, g/cm^3, rupees), chemical formulas (H2O, CO2, C6H12O6: element symbols checked against a list, never a guess), a few Greek letters, brackets that hold maths, and technical terms.
3. **Registers.** `ncert` is textbook Hindi: numbers in Hindi words (0 to 99 each their own word, then सौ, हज़ार, लाख, करोड़), signs धन, ऋण, गुणा, भाग, बराबर, `x का वर्ग`, `तीन बटा चार`, `दो दशमलव पाँच`. `hinglish` is the classroom mix: English number and operator words (`x square`, `three by four`, `minus five`) inside a Hindi sentence. Nepali has one register of its own (the `register` argument is accepted and ignored). Only the language parts become Devanagari; everything else of the author's sentence is left as written.
4. **Two open readings of the owner's table, both switchable.** The proposal reads 0 to 10 in Hindi in the hinglish register, but its own worked examples (`minus five`, `three by four`, `two point five`) read them in English. The default follows the examples; `smallNumbers: "hindi"` gives the other reading. Numbers of 100000 and more use lakh and crore in English too (Indian English), not million. The owner decides both.
5. **English words and Devanagari.** The hinglish register keeps its English words in Latin letters by default. `englishScript: "devanagari"` writes them as Devanagari (`translit` table in the lexicon, 106 words including months and ordinals) for a vendor that cannot read Latin script. Chemical formulas are always letter names in Devanagari (एच टू ओ), because a voice cannot be trusted with the Latin letters of a formula. The `ncert` and Nepali registers use the lexicon's Devanagari term (`quadratic` becomes द्विघात); the hinglish register keeps the English term, or its transliteration, as the lexicon says.
6. **The lexicon is data.** `lexicon.json` holds every word the normaliser can say: one entry per sign, structure, unit and term with `hinglish`, `ncert` and `ne` wording and a `needsNativeCheck` flag per language. A reviewer edits that file; no code change. A test fails if an `ncert` or Nepali entry contains Latin letters. **Every Nepali entry is flagged `needsNativeCheck: true`.** A Nepali entry with `"ne": null` (+, -, ×, ÷, =, the relations, the arrow, the unary plus, slash) has no wording: the output carries a visible `NE_TODO_<id>` marker and the entry is listed in `pending`, with a hint in `neSuggestion` that is never used in output. Nepali is therefore not releasable until the list is empty. The Nepali number words (0 to 99), ordinals, month names and letter names were written from memory and are flagged as whole tables.
7. **Pauses.** After each equation (a run of maths pieces that holds `=` or another relation) the output carries `[pause 0.6s]`, the marker form of ADR 0096 and 0097, with the pause configurable (`pauseMs`, clamped to the marker's 200 ms to 3 s, 0 turns it off). The voice step has no mid-sentence pause (ADR 0097 left it unbuilt), so `spokenMathForVoice` removes the markers and adds the longest as silence after the sentence.
8. **Behind a flag, off by default.** `options.spokenMath` (`"hinglish"` or `"ncert"`) on a story job. `scripts/story-video.ts` applies it only when the locale is `hi-IN` or `ne-NP`; absent, the code path is the one that was there (`speechText` alone). The option is left out of `storyOutputOptions` unless set, so existing build keys do not change (ADR 0003, ADR 0099 item 6); the contract line in `ENGINE_INTERFACE.md` lists it. The English path is not touched.
9. **Review tooling, no audio.** `pnpm voice:review-sheet <lesson>` writes a CSV (UTF-8 with a byte-order mark) and a plain-text checklist: per sentence the original, the spoken text, the lexicon entries used and a blank column for each of six checks (numbers, signs, terms, register, pause, naturalness) plus a pass / fix / re-record verdict and a corrected-wording column. `pnpm voice:lexicon-pending` lists every entry a native speaker must check or write. The six checks are the engineers' reading of the owner's "10-minute checklist"; the owner may replace them.

## Tests
- The owner's worked table, asserted exactly for Hindi (both registers) and for the Nepali entries the owner wrote.
- A regression set of 284 invented strings (numbers from 0 to 1,000,000, fractions, mixed numbers, negatives, decimals, powers, roots, equations, units, percent, formulas, ranges, ordinals, dates, terms, mixed prose) with exact expected Hindi output in both registers. The expected outputs were read by an engineer; they are not yet checked by a native speaker.
- The same strings in Nepali: not the wording, but that no ASCII digit or maths symbol survives, that the number of equations and pauses matches the Hindi, and that every pending entry has no wording. The same no-survivor check runs in every register and script and for every integer from 0 to 1000.
- The flag: absent from a parsed request and from the build key unless set; `speechText` unchanged.

## What this does not do (deferred)
- **TTS vendor choice.** No vendor is wired. The bake-off (Sarvam Bulbul v3, ElevenLabs v3, and for Nepali whatever is tested) is a separate decision and needs paid calls the owner has not authorised.
- **Narrator recording.** The Nepali native narrator, and any Hindi re-records a reviewer asks for.
- **The listening-test protocol** (a MOS of at least 4.0 and its panel and scripts) is not designed here.
- **Native review.** No Hindi or Nepali wording is approved. The regression set freezes the engineers' proposal so that a reviewer's change shows up as a deliberate test change.
- **Devanagari on screen.** Checked read-only: the ffmpeg the engine uses (`ffmpeg-static` 5.3.0, FFmpeg 6.1.1) is built with libharfbuzz, libfreetype, libfribidi and libass, and its `drawtext` filter has `text_shaping` (default on). The engine does not use `drawtext`: pages are drawn by Chromium and ffmpeg only encodes them, so what matters for on-screen Devanagari is the font in the page. The Atkinson Hyperlegible face of ADR 0098 has no Devanagari glyphs, so Hindi and Nepali text falls back to whatever the browser finds. A Devanagari face (for example Noto Sans Devanagari) and the digits style of ADR 0006 need a decision and a render check. The Linux build used on the deploy host was not checked.
- **Word order of relations.** `x < 5` is read in the written order; Hindi and Nepali would put the second operand first (5 से छोटा x). A reviewer decides the phrasing; the entries say so.
- **Mid-sentence pauses** stay unbuilt (ADR 0097): the pause for an equation is added after its sentence.
- **Lesson bundles.** The hook is on story jobs only; the verified voice bundles of lessons (ADR 0084) are left alone for the reason ADR 0099 item 5 gives.

## Consequences
- A story in Hindi or Nepali can have its maths spoken from words the engine chose, not words a vendor chose, once the flag is on and a reviewer has approved the lexicon.
- The wording is one JSON file; a reviewer's change is a data change with a test diff.
- Nepali stays blocked by design until a native speaker fills the empty entries: an unfinished table is visible in the output instead of being guessed.
