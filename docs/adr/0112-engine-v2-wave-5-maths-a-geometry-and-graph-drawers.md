# ADR 0112: Engine V2 wave 5 (Maths A: coordinates, graphs of functions, the number line, plane geometry, solids, transformations, vectors and 3-D points)

Status: Proposed, 2026-11-12. The owner accepts it. Builds on ADR 0100 (the verified figure layer, of which this wave builds the drawing half and uses the existing verifier for the rest), ADR 0107 (scope, wave plan, licence policy, privacy rule), ADR 0108 (the drawing kit, read-back, the unreviewed-drawer switch), ADR 0110 (the whole-grid figure, the per-kind request) and ADR 0111 (the corruption-test pattern). Touches no frozen file and no item of section 15 of the architecture. `docs/ENGINE_INTERFACE.md` is unchanged.

## Context
ADR 0107 puts Maths in waves 5 and 6 for Classes 8 to 12 (`OBJECT-COVERAGE-8-12.md`, section 5.2, W5 and W6). The owner asked on 2026-11-12 for the first half of the maths waves to be the half that is mostly geometry and graphs: coordinate geometry, graphs of functions (linear, quadratic, polynomial, trigonometric, exponential and logarithmic), the number line and inequalities, geometry constructions and theorems (triangles, circles, congruence and similarity, tangents, angles, polygons, the solids of mensuration with their nets and cross-sections), transformations, vectors and three-dimensional geometry. Statistics, probability, calculus, matrices and sequences are left to wave 6. The method is the one of waves 1 to 4: pure TypeScript, no new dependency, computed numbers from tested pure functions, read-back from the drawn shapes only, licence owner-original, deterministic, flags off by default, every drawer `reviewed: false` (the reviewer is not a build blocker, ADR 0108 item 6).

ADR 0100 says a figure that is slightly wrong is worse than no figure, and that a maths figure's numbers are proved by SymPy. The wave-1 to wave-4 drawers did not use SymPy (the science figures do not need it). Here it applies to some of the figures and not to others, because the verifier's expression language is closed to `num sym neg add sub mul div pow eq` (no square root, trigonometry, logarithm, pi or inequality). Item 6 below says which figures go through it and which cannot.

## Decision
1. **Which drawers.** 22 drawers in `packages/pipeline/objects/maths/`, one per figure of the request: coordinates (points and quadrants, distance, midpoint, section point, area by coordinates; `m_coord`), a straight line and a pair of lines (slope, intercepts, angle of inclination, two points, intercept form, intersection, parallel, same line; `m_line`), graphs of functions (quadratic with vertex, axis and roots, cubic from its roots, exponential, logarithm, rational with asymptotes; `m_fnplot`), sine, cosine and tangent with amplitude and period (`m_trig`), the unit circle at an angle (`m_unitcircle`), the number line (a linear inequality solved with the sign turning round, an interval with open and closed ends, marks; `m_numline`), a triangle from SSS, SAS or ASA with one of its four centres (`m_triangle`), the right triangle with Pythagoras, the trigonometric ratios and optional squares on the sides (`m_right`), similar triangles, congruent triangles and the basic proportionality theorem (`m_congsim`), a circle with a chord, the tangents from a point, the angle at the centre and on the circle, a cyclic quadrilateral (`m_circle`), the six quadrilaterals with their diagonals and properties (`m_quad`), a regular polygon with its angles and lines of symmetry (`m_polygon`), angles (a transversal across parallel lines with the pair named, the angle sum of a triangle, the exterior angle; `m_angles`), a sector and a segment (`m_sector`), ruler-and-compass constructions (perpendicular bisector, angle bisector, equilateral triangle; `m_construct`), heights and distances (angle of elevation and of depression; `m_elevation`), the six solids with dimensions (`m_solid`), the nets of a cuboid, a cylinder and a cone (`m_net`), the cross-section of a sphere, a cone and a cylinder (`m_section`), reflection, turn, translation and dilation of a triangle (`m_transform`), two-dimensional vectors (sum, components, dot and cross product; `m_vector`) and a point or a pair of points in three dimensions with the distance and the direction cosines (`m_space`). They cover 22 of the plan's maths objects by name, and one more (cross-sections) that the inventory does not name. Three of the 22 are objects the plan puts in wave 6 (solids, nets, vectors and 3-D points), which the owner asked to have with geometry; the plan's rows count them in wave 6 and the coverage report says so.
2. **Own geometry only, no data.** Every drawer is pure TypeScript, licence `owner-original`, source `own-drawings`, no tool, no `toolVersions`, no new dependency; the source register is unchanged. Pictures are lines, polygons, circles, ellipses, rectangles, paths with arcs and dots. There are no data of record in this wave: the only constants are the ones of a projection (`SKEW` 0.35 for the 3-D axes, a depth edge half the length at 45 degrees for a cuboid, an ellipse a third as high as it is wide for a circle seen from above).
3. **One request kind.** A new object kind `maths` carries `figure` (the closed list above), `params` and `lang` (`en`). `params` are numbers, booleans and closed lists, checked per figure by a strict schema in `maths/params.ts`: there is no formula string and no free text, so nothing a learner typed can ride in. The figure takes the whole stage grid, A1:F6; a smaller area is refused at parse time (its picture, scale bar and column of read-outs are laid out for that size).
4. **Impossible inputs are refused with the rule they break.** A triangle whose sides break the triangle inequality (and two angles that leave no room for a third, and a similar or congruent triangle on such sides); a leg longer than its hypotenuse; a tangent length from a point inside the circle, and from a point on it; a chord that is as far from the centre as the radius or farther; a plane that does not cut a sphere, a cone or a cylinder; an angle sum of a triangle that is not 180 (three angles given that do not add up, two that already do); a transversal at 90 degrees, where the pairs cannot be told apart; a pair of lines said to meet that are parallel, or the same line, or the reverse; a vertical line, a line steeper than 10, an intercept form with an intercept of 0; a quadratic said to have two roots, one root or none when its discriminant says otherwise, and a quadratic with a = 0; a base of 1 for an exponential or a logarithm; a rational function with k = 0; roots out of order; an inequality whose solution set is empty (a left end above the right end, equal ends with an open one), one with a = 0 (no boundary), marks at one place; three collinear points given as a triangle or as a triangle to be transformed; a dilation by 0 or by 1, a translation by (0, 0), a whole number of turns; two vectors that cancel or a vector of length 0; the origin as a point whose direction cosines are asked; a frustum whose radii are equal; a trapezium whose parallel sides are equal. The router turns any refusal into a note and no drawing.
5. **Truth from computed numbers.** Every number is computed by a tested pure function in `maths/compute.ts` and printed once as `key = value unit`, to three significant figures, with the noise of floating point taken off first (30.000000000000004 prints as 30.0; a count or a whole number without a unit prints whole). Words that carry a number ("(2, 3), quadrant 1", "x at most 2.25") are built from the same computed numbers. The tests check each function against a known value, an identity or a brute-force check over seeded random inputs (`tests/objects-maths-compute.test.ts`): the section point against the ratio of distances; the shoelace area against half the cross product; the quadratic roots against the equation, the sum and product of the roots and a scan for the vertex; a clipped line against a scan of x; a linear inequality, with its turning round, against a grid of 160 values of x for 400 random cases; the three ways of fixing a triangle against the law of sines and each other; Heron against 3-4-5, 13-14-15 and the shoelace; each of the four centres against its definition (equal distance from the vertices, from the sides, perpendicular from each vertex) and Euler's line; Pythagoras and the three ways of giving a right triangle; the basic proportionality theorem against parallel directions and the area ratio; a chord and a tangent against the coordinates of the points; a cyclic quadrilateral placed by its arcs against the measured angles; a regular polygon against its angles and area; the six quadrilaterals against the properties of their names; the area of a sector and a segment against a fine polygon; the volumes and curved surface areas of the cone, the frustum, the sphere, the hemisphere and the cylinder against Simpson integration; the fold of a cone's net (the arc is the circumference of the base); the isometries against distances; the dot and cross products against the angle; the direction cosines against 1.
6. **The existing verifier, where it applies.** The facts of 14 of the 22 figures that the closed expression language can state are written as claims for the SymPy verifier by `maths/claims.ts` (`figureClaims`): the roots of a quadratic or cubic, their sum and product, the discriminant and the vertex; the intercepts and the meeting point of lines and the slope from two points; the midpoint, the section point and the square of a distance; the area by coordinates; Pythagoras for a right triangle; the square of the tangent length and of the half chord; the angle sum, each interior and exterior angle and the diagonals of a polygon; the third angle and the sum of a triangle; the boundary of a linear inequality; the ratio of the areas of similar triangles; the volume, surface area and diagonal of a cuboid; the squared distance in space; a vector sum and a dot product; a translation and a dilation; Heron's formula. The test sends them to `verifyBundle` of `@eve/verify`, the same SymPy process the lessons use, in one run for the 54 sample figures that have claims (122 facts, all verified), shows that making one stated number wrong by one fails every one of those figures, and runs 150 random cases (quadratics with whole-number roots, line pairs, section points). The drawers do not call the verifier (it is a Python process and a drawer is pure and fast): a wrong number in `compute.ts` is caught by the test, not at drawing time. The verifier was right to refuse a claim the first draft made: a quadratic with no real root has complex roots, which `roots` states over the complex numbers, so such a figure is stated by its exact discriminant and the roots are listed as omitted. **Not in the verifier's language, so not verified by it:** sine, cosine and tangent (graphs and unit circle), exponential and logarithm, every quantity that is irrational (a square root, pi) including the chord, the tangent length itself, the diagonals, the hypotenuse of most triangles and everything about a circle's area or a cylinder's or cone's volume, the direction cosines, the angles of a triangle from its sides, the ratio of a sector, the section of a cone or sphere as a radius, heights from an angle, the rotation by an angle that is not a multiple of 90, the direction of an inequality (the language has no inequality) and the geometry of a drawing (a construction, a net, the position of a point on a picture); `NOT_EXPRESSIBLE` in `claims.ts` names each of the 8 figures with no claim and why. For those, the check is the read-back and the tests of item 5 against independent computations, not SymPy; adding a square root, trigonometric and logarithm handler to the verifier (each with its SymPy handler and the rule that keeps the two lists equal, ADR 0100 item F3) is the step that would change this and is not made here.
7. **Read-back (Q4).** Each drawer's `readBack` reads only the drawn shapes. A figure on axes prints the range of each axis (as in wave 1) and the read-back turns the drawn points into values with it: a coordinate figure reads each point, its label and the segment joining them; a line is read from its two end points against the clipped line the inputs give and from its two intercept marks; a graph is compared point by point with the closed form at the sample positions the plan lists, asymptotes are read from their lines and no branch may cross one; the unit circle reads cos and sin from the drawn point and the two drawn legs and the sweep of the wedge from its polygon; the number line reads the circle (open or closed), the direction of the ray and the label, and compares them with the inequality solved again from the inputs, so a ray that was not turned round is caught. A figure on a scale bar (triangles, circles, polygons, solids, constructions, heights) carries a bar of a whole number of units at a scale that is a multiple of 0.05 stage units per unit, exact to the drawn precision, and the read-back turns every drawn length into a length: the sides, angles and area of a triangle (and the position of its centre, recomputed from the drawn vertices); Pythagoras on the drawn sides; the squares on them; a drawn chord's distance and half length; a tangent's right angle at the point of contact and the equal tangents; the angle on the circle and the sweep of the wedge at the centre; the four angles of a cyclic quadrilateral measured on its vertices; the diagonals and the properties of a quadrilateral from its drawn vertices (with a tolerance for the rounding of a drawn point); the corners, sides, angles and the axes of symmetry of a polygon (reflecting every corner in a drawn axis must give the corners again); the angle marks of a transversal against the drawn lines and the pair; a sector's arc flag, radius and angle from its path; the perpendicular bisector and the angle bisector from the circles drawn (the line must pass through the two points where the drawn circles meet); the edges of a cuboid from its front face and its depth edge, the radius and height of a cylinder, a cone or a frustum from their ellipses, and the volume and total surface area recomputed from those drawn dimensions; a net folds (a cylinder's rectangle is as wide as the circumference of the drawn circles, a cone's arc as long as the circumference of its base circle); a section's chord against Pythagoras or similar triangles; the steps of the staircase of a 3-D point decoded through the projection; a transformation recomputed from the drawn triangle (the mirror line must be the perpendicular bisector of each point and its image, a turn must keep the distance from the drawn centre and turn by the angle, a dilation must put each image on the ray at k times the distance). The corruption set is `tests/maths-corrupt.ts` plus the generic one of wave 1: every sample rejects every corruption that applies to it, and at least two apply (1142 checks over the 131 samples, at least five for each). Writing the set found 40 of the 131 samples whose read-back missed something (a label, a mark, a segment, the scale bar) and each was fixed.
8. **The switch is unchanged.** All wave-5 drawers are registered `reviewed: false`; the router asks `ENGINE_OBJECTS_ALLOW_UNREVIEWED` as ADR 0108 item 6 says. Coverage counts registered and reviewed pairs apart. The objects flags stay off by default and with `ENGINE_OBJECTS_V2` off the output is byte-identical (test Q1).
9. **Accessibility, languages.** As ADR 0108 items 8 and 9: text of at least 2.4 stage units in the safe label alphabet, strokes only in ink, soft, red and purple (3:1 contrast tested), fills light (opacity at most 0.5), the dots of a mark at most 1.7 across. A figure and its image are told apart by a dash and by their own letters (A, A'), never by colour alone. Labels pass through `LABEL_HOOKS`; the closed alphabet has no minus sign at the start of a text, no `<`, `>` or `|`, so the number-line labels read `x=-2`, an inequality is written in words (greater than, at least, less than, at most) and a length is `Length of a`. A label is placed by a small search for a free spot (eight directions at two distances); if none is free the wrapper's overlap check refuses the figure, so a crowded figure fails closed.
10. **Shared files.** New code is in `maths/`. Additive edits: `spec.ts` (the kind `maths`, its parse branch, the minimum area), `drawers.ts` (registers the 22), `index.ts` (re-export), `scripts/objects-sheet.ts` (`--maths`), `scripts/objects-coverage.ts` (a wave-5 line), and in the tests only `objects-spec.test.ts` (the example of each kind) and the two lines of `objects-physics-gates.test.ts` that counted every registered drawer as a physics one. The registration goes through the wave-1 wrapper (`kindDrawer`), so a figure keeps to 24 shapes (the largest sample has 23) and a scene holds one maths figure. The samples file is `fixtures/objects/maths-w5-samples.json`; the inspector's Objects panel reads every `*-samples.json` of that folder and the registry, so the 131 figures, the 22 drawers and the coverage numbers appear in it with no edit to the panel (the panel groups drawers by the part of the file name before `-wave`, which this file's name does not have, so the maths drawers are listed under its group "other"; renaming the group is a one-word change to the panel and is left to its owner).

## Data of record used
None. Conventions used, stated in `compute.ts`: angles in degrees at the edges and in radians inside; y up on a graph; a triangle has A at the origin and B on the x axis; the right angle of a right triangle is at C; the oblique projection of a 3-D point is (y - 0.35 x, z - 0.35 x); the unit circle has an angle measured anticlockwise from the positive x axis; the circle seen from above is an ellipse 0.3 times as high as it is wide. The school value of pi is `Math.PI`.

## What this wave does not do
- **Not drawn (deferred to wave 6, as the owner said):** statistics (bar chart, histogram, frequency polygon, pie chart, mean, median and mode, box plot, scatter, ogive), probability (trees, sample-space grids, simulation), counting trees and the binomial triangle, calculus (limits and the secant to the tangent, the tangent line and the derivative graph, the area under a curve, slope fields), matrices and determinants, sequences (arithmetic and geometric terms and staircase bars), the Argand diagram, conic sections, linear programming and the shading of an inequality in the plane, inverse trigonometric graphs, mapping diagrams, proofs and truth tables, flowcharts, sets and Venn diagrams. **Not drawn, from the plan's wave 5 and outside the half the owner chose:** square and cube models, fraction and ratio bars, the area model of an algebraic identity, the real-number tree and the square-root spiral, factor trees and prime grids, the balance scale, equation steps, place-value charts and tables (the last four are drawn by the existing path).
- **Not drawn, inside the half:** the secant of a circle, the tangent-chord and alternate-segment angles, the circle through three points as a figure in itself, a circle in the coordinate plane, parabola, ellipse and hyperbola, the graph of a quadratic inequality, trigonometric identities and the sum-of-angles graphs, inverse functions and the reflection of a graph in y = x, a polynomial of degree four or more, absolute value and modulus graphs, piecewise functions, the graph of a straight line given by a general equation with a vertical line, lines and planes in three dimensions beyond a point and a distance (no line equation, no plane, no angle between lines), the cross product of vectors in three dimensions and a vector in three dimensions as an arrow, a perspective or rotating view of any solid, the net of a pyramid or a prism other than a cuboid, composite solids and a solid with a hole, composite plane figures, the incircle and circumcircle as figures beyond the triangle's own centre option, the nine-point circle, Euler's line as a drawn line, symmetry of a figure other than a regular polygon, a glide reflection, a composition of two transformations, a shear, tiling, a loci figure, bearings, a map scale, three-figure heights and distances with two observers, and a diagram of a word problem beyond heights and distances.
- **Limits of what is drawn:** one figure per scene, on the whole grid. A construction is drawn with full dashed circles instead of arcs of them (the picture is crowded, but the circles' centres and radii can be read back), and the order of the steps is only the order the shapes appear in, not a cue of its own for each step. The unit circle is static (the plan's "live sine and cosine" needs motion, which `ENGINE_MOTION_V2` does not yet do). The angle marks are small polygons, not arcs. A solid is in a fixed oblique or elevation view, hidden edges dashed, with a fixed tilt of the ellipses. The number line labels only the marked values (a tick label with a minus sign is not in the closed alphabet); its range is printed in words. A sine or cosine is drawn over two periods and a tangent over two periods in three branches that stop short of the asymptote at the height where |y| is 3 A, so the branches are drawn to a clipped height, not to infinity. A rational function is drawn to a clipped height as well. A cuboid net is the one cross layout of four faces in a strip and two ends. Inputs are whole or decimal numbers in stated ranges (lengths 0.5 to 200, coordinates and vectors a few tens, angles in degrees); a figure whose labels would overlap is refused by the layout check and the router shows a label card.
- **Not verified:** see item 6 for what SymPy does not prove. In addition: the drawings' labels (letters at the vertices) are checked for the right letter at the nearest vertex, not for the right idea; the plan's chapter lists remain `UNVERIFIED` against the official syllabus; a handful of figures look crowded in a frame (the letters A and B of the angle bisector sit on its arms, P1 of the 3-D distance touches the z axis, the angle marks of a transversal are coarse polygons); they pass the layout check, and a reviewer may want them moved. The frames were looked at in real Chromium for 16 sample frames and 4 frames of 3 of the 6 story requests (the rest were drawn without error and read back, not looked at). No live model call, network or paid service was used.
- **Not reviewed:** no maths teacher has looked at any figure. The reviewer is `[owner to name]`. Every drawer is `reviewed: false`.
- Motion rules (`ENGINE_MOTION_V2`) and the mistake visuals of the Class 10 Board Sprint are untouched.

## Consequences
- The maths objects of the geometry and graphs half of W5 can be drawn in development and staging now; production keeps label cards until the owner flips the switch or records reviews.
- Coverage by name counts an object as covered once a drawer for part of it is registered; the list above says which parts are not drawn, and the gap list (`pnpm objects:coverage <inventory> --gaps`) names the objects no drawer covers. Against the owner's inventory file the report gives maths 77 of 155 pairs registered (wave 5 66 of 95, wave 6 11 of 60), none reviewed; the plan's own "drawn today" grade adds the pairs it counts as drawn by the existing path.
- Wave 6 starts from here: the figure layer (a frame, a scale bar, the label search, the read-back helpers and the corruption set) is reusable by a histogram, a probability tree or a matrix, and the verifier handlers for the square root, trigonometry and logarithm are the step that would move more of the figures from "read back and tested" to "proved by SymPy".
