# ADR 0114: Video rules: colour roles, template notes and a captions sidecar

Status: Proposed, 2026-11-18. The owner approved the work; the owner accepts the ADR. Builds on ADR 0091, 0096 to 0099. Touches no frozen file and no item of section 15 of the architecture. `docs/ENGINE_INTERFACE.md` changes additively (contract v0, minor 0.1).

## Context
A learning-design review of the story videos (coherence, signalling, segmenting; one idea per clip; the same colour for the same meaning; no decorative graphics; captions on by default with a toggle) found three gaps in the story path (`packages/pipeline/job/story*.ts`, `scripts/story-runtime.js`, the path the host's video jobs use): the palettes have no rule about what a colour means; the QA checks that a hook ends with a question but not the rest of a lesson's shape; and the captions are burned into the picture, so a student-facing toggle is impossible. The host's story prompts are not changed by this ADR.

## Decision
Each part is additive. When it is not used, the pictures, the page and the files are byte for byte what they were (test `story-roles.test.ts` holds the hashes of the sanitised story, the placed story, the QA notes and the page in four styles, taken from the engine before the change; a real render before and after is recorded in the pull request).

1. **Colour roles.** Four roles: `term` (the thing being explained), `key` (the key element or result), `look-again` (amber: a caution or common mistake), `checked` (green: verified by code). A shape takes an optional `role`; a scene takes an optional `highlights: [{ s, word, role }]` (a word of a sentence, drawn bold, underlined and in the role colour in the burned-in caption, at most 4 a scene, dropped with a note when the word is not in the sentence). `story-roles.ts` holds one colour per role for each palette (whiteboard, dark, animated), the same in every scene. Contrast, computed from the hexes in the test: at least 4.5:1 against every background the palette puts under text (above 5.1 for all twelve colours) and so at least 3:1 for graphics. No meaning rests on hue alone: a caution is drawn dashed and a caution or a check starts its label or text with "!" or "OK" (a formula shape has no text to prefix; the dash and the colour carry it). The role colours reach the page only for a story that names a role (`role_term`, `role_key`, `role_look_again`, `role_checked` in the page palette); the runtime is not changed. An unknown role is dropped with a note; an absent role changes nothing. A QA note (advisory, never an error) says when the colours the author wrote disagree about a role (`role_colour_varies`, `role_colour_shared`; the engine's default colour for a kind is not counted as written).
2. **Template notes.** `storyAdvisories` in `story-qa.ts`, apart from `storyQA` so that the notes of an existing story do not change: `key_term_unmarked`, `key_term_unnamed`, `scene_many_ideas`, `worked_example_missing`, `worked_example_unmarked`, `recap_missing`, `recap_long`, `handoff_missing`. The heuristics are in the comment above the function: more than one sentence that defines a term ("is called", "we call", ...) or more than five different labelled shapes make "more than one idea" (`IDEA_LIMITS`); a worked example is a scene whose heading or sentence says example, for instance, suppose, let us try; a recap is the wrap scene and is long when it has more than one sentence besides its hand-off; a hand-off is a sentence with "your turn", "try one", "now you", "can you solve" and the like. English cues apply only to `en-IN`. They are warnings: they never change a verdict. `scripts/video-job.ts` appends them to `qa.json` as entries `{ id: "advisory:<kind>", ok: true, severity: "advisory", kind, scene, message }` after the delivery checks; the pass or fail of a job is decided from the checks without `severity: "advisory"` as before (the job's `qa` event still counts the delivery checks only). `qa.json` of a story job therefore gains entries; no other output changes. The interface document says a consumer must ignore a kind it does not know.
3. **Captions sidecar.** `options.captions` takes `sidecar` beside `sentence` (the default, burned in, unchanged) and `none`. With `sidecar` the page is drawn without the caption band and `story-captions.ts` writes `captions.vtt` beside `lesson.mp4`: one cue per sentence at the sentence's own start (the time the burned-in caption starts to show it), ending when the speech ends, at least one second, never past the next sentence or the end of the scene; a sentence over two lines of 42 characters is split into consecutive two-line cues; the text is cleaned (control characters removed, `&`, `<`, `>` escaped). `result.json` gains `captions: { file, language, format }` (language is the job locale) and `files.captions`, only for `sidecar`. The idempotency and build keys already hold the value of `captions`, so a request that does not set it keeps its hashes (test), and a `sidecar` request builds on its own.
4. **The hand-off promise.** The YourTutor player will show captions from the sidecar with a toggle that is on by default. Burned-in captions remain the default until YourTutor opts in by sending `captions: "sidecar"`; the engine never changes the default on its own. YourTutor's own ADR records the adoption (`docs/ENGINE_INTEGRATION.md` there), as the workflow requires for a contract change. The host that asks for `sidecar` is responsible for showing the captions (a `<track kind="captions">` with the language from `result.json`), because the video then has none.

## Consequences
- Tests: `story-roles.test.ts` (before-and-after hashes, contrast of every role in every palette, the pairing, roles on shapes and words, the advisory kinds), `story-captions.test.ts` (WebVTT validity with a small reader, parity with the burned-in timing, the schema and the unchanged build key).
- Not changed: the host's story prompts, the runtime script, the lesson (knowledge-base) path, the player. The role palette does not follow a per-scene `bg` colour the author chose: contrast is guaranteed on the palette's own backgrounds only.
- Risks: a sidecar video has no caption until the host shows the track; the heuristics of the template notes are English and rough (they advise, they do not judge); a role colour is a new colour, so a story that mixes role and plain colours sees both.
