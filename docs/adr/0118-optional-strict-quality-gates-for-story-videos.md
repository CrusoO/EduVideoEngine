# ADR 0118: Optional strict quality gates for story videos

Status: Proposed, 2026-11-23. The owner accepts the ADR. Builds on ADR 0101, 0102, 0114, 0115 and 0116. Touches no frozen file and no item of section 15 of the architecture. `docs/ENGINE_INTERFACE.md` changes additively (section 10): one optional request option and some qa.json entries; a request that does not use it is read, keyed and built as before.

## Context
The soft notes of a story (`noun_undrawn`, `dead_air`, `cell_conflict`, the layout notes) are advisory: `ok` is always true, so a video with text on top of text, a formula in the caption band or a narrated wall that is never drawn still ships. ADR 0116 listed "hard quality gates" as not done. A research pass (the report "Dynamic video engine for any object", section "Hard gates in code, soft notes from models") says which checks can be gates: geometry and arithmetic are exact; a model's judgement of a picture is not reliable enough to fail a job on. A host that wants a hard guarantee (for a video it will not look at) needs a switch, and a host that does not ask must see no change.

## Decision
A request option `options.qa: "strict"` (the only value; absent means today's behaviour). When it is set on a `story` job, after the build (the placed story, after the automatic layout, is in `story/timeline.json`) and before the render, the job runs these checks with pure code (`job/story-gates.ts`, `strictGates`; no browser, no clock, no model). Any failure ends the job as `qa_failed` before the render is paid for. Without the option nothing in this ADR runs.

1. **Occupancy table (advisory, always written when strict).** For every shape with a measurable box (the same `boxOf` as the layout: circles, rectangles, props, text and formulas, with the end of its moves; shapes placed by an expression of time, lines, arrows and plots have no row): `{ scene, shape, kind, cells, sentence, text, fromMs, toMs }`, where `cells` is the 6 by 6 grid area (ADR 0102) the box covers (`B2` or `A1:C3`), `sentence` the sentence that starts the shape (from 0) with its `text`, and the window is when it is on screen (its start to its end, or the end of the scene). Written to qa.json as one entry `advisory:occupancy` with `severity: "advisory"` (it never fails).
2. **Overlap.** A line of text or a formula that overlaps another shape while both are on screen (area above 0.15 stage unit squared, the layout's own threshold). Same allow-list as the layout: a label inside a box, circle or prop that stays put (90 percent inside) is fine, and so is anything over a line, an arrow or a spring. Two lines of text are reported once, for the later one. The helpers (`boxOf`, `overlaps`, `area`, `inside`, `holds`, `obstacle`) are those of `story-layout.ts`, now exported; their behaviour is unchanged.
3. **Safe area and caption band.** No measured shape outside x 2 to 98, y 1.5 to 42 (`SAFE`), which also keeps everything above the caption band that starts at y 43 (tolerance 0.05). A rectangle that is at least 95 percent of the stage wide or tall is a backdrop (sky, ground) and may touch the edge.
4. **Text size on a phone.** The frame is 1920 px wide and a 390 px phone shows it at 390/1920 = 0.2031. A unit of the stage is 16.8 frame px on the card page (`chrome: "full"`, the stage is 1680 px for 100 units) and 19.2 frame px full-bleed (`chrome: "none"`, which the animated style defaults to). The minimum is **10 px on the phone**, which is 10 x 1920 / 390 = 49.23 frame px, so a `text` or `math` `size` must be at least 49.23 / 16.8 = **2.93** units (card) or 49.23 / 19.2 = **2.56** units (full-bleed). Why 10 and not the more usual 12: 12 px needs 3.52 and 3.08 units, and the engine's own caption (34 to 40 frame px) is 6.9 to 8.1 px on the same phone, so a rule of 12 would hold hosts to a standard the engine does not meet itself; 10 is a floor for a line a student must read, not a target. The default text size, 2.4, is 8.2 px and fails: a host that asks for strict QA has to size its text. The constant is `MIN_PHONE_PX`; the owner may raise it (an ADR amendment and a changed test).
5. **Contrast (WCAG 2.2).** (a) Every colour of the role palette in use (`ROLE_PALETTES`, by theme and style) against the colours under the stage text (the theme's `bg` and `panel`, with the animated overrides) is at least 4.5:1. (b) Every `text` and `math` shape against the background of its scene (the scene's own solid colour or both ends of its gradient when it names one in `#rrggbb`) is at least 4.5:1, or 3:1 when large. Large is 24 px or more on the phone-scaled frame, or 18.66 px or more and bold (WCAG's 18 pt and 14 pt bold), computed at the card scale (the stricter). With these rules text almost never counts as large; that is intended. (c) Every line, arrow and spring is at least 3:1. A shape with a `role` uses its role colour, otherwise its named colour (`ink`, `blue`, ...); `none` is skipped. Fills are not checked (every drawn shape has the charcoal outline), nor a label's own colour. The ratio is `contrastRatio` of `story-roles.ts`. Today's role colours on white are 6.62 (term), 6.87 (key), 6.06 (look-again) and 5.43 (checked), asserted in the test; the palette's yellow (`#d99a00`) and orange on the whiteboard do not reach 4.5:1 for text, so strict QA refuses them, which is the point.
6. **Noun drawn at its cue.** The structural form of the soft `noun_undrawn` (same `PROP_NAMES` and `PROP_WORDS`): for the first sentence of a scene that names a thing with a drawn prop, a shape must be on screen within 500 ms of the time that sentence starts: a prop of that name, or a shape whose label or text uses the word, with opacity above 0.5, that has appeared by then (`inMs <= t + 500`) and has not gone (`outMs >= t - 500`). Later sentences that repeat the word are not checked.
7. **Unapproved: not applicable yet.** The engine has no approver field (ADR 0116 records `approvedBy: null`). No check and no field are invented here; a later ADR that adds an approver can add this gate.

**Output.** qa.json gains one entry for each check, `{ id: "strict:overlap" | "strict:safe_area" | "strict:text_size" | "strict:contrast" | "strict:noun_at_cue", ok, message, failures? }` (`failures`, present when `ok` is false, is a list of `{ check, scene, shape?, sentence?, message }`), then the entries the job always wrote, then `advisory:occupancy`. A failed job ends with stage `qa`, code `qa_failed`, a `detail` such as `strict QA failed: overlap (2), text_size (1)`, a qa.json that holds the strict entries and the occupancy table, and a `result.json` with `qaFailures`: the list of every failure. A passing job is unchanged apart from the extra entries.

**Keys.** `qa` is in the request schema as an optional enum and nowhere else. It is not part of `storyOutputOptions`, the build key or the render key, because it changes no frame and no sound: the same story builds to the same bytes with or without it, and a strict request reuses a finished build. The request hash (idempotency) does include it, so a strict request is a different request from the same one without it, as it should be.

## Consequences
- Tests (`story-gates.test.ts`): a good story that passes every check; one corrupted fixture for each check that fails on its own and the matching good one; the phone arithmetic; the contrast numbers; the occupancy table; the checks do not change the story; and the guard hashes of the parsed request, the story output options and the build key of a request without `qa`, taken from the engine before the change (the existing hashes in `story-roles.test.ts` are not touched and still pass).
- The wiring in `scripts/video-job.ts` (read `placed` from `timeline.json`, run, write qa.json, fail) is a success-and-failure path of a real story job; a job needs the voice and (for a pass) the browser render, so it was not run in the session that wrote this ADR (see the pull request).
- Risks: the width of text is an estimate (ADR 0115), so an overlap or a safe-area result near the line can differ from the picture; a shape placed by an expression of time is not measured; the strict contrast rules refuse most of the palette's accent colours for text, so a host has to use `ink` or a role; the 10 px floor is a choice, not a standard; a gate fails the job, so a host that turns it on must be ready to repair and resend (the failure list names the scene, shape and sentence for that).
- Not built: a pixel check of the rendered frames (OCR or contrast sampled from a screenshot), occlusion by z-order, a gate on a source (`source`) video, `qa` for the lesson path.
