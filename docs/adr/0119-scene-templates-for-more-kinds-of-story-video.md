# ADR 0119: Scene templates for more kinds of story video

Status: Proposed, 2026-11-27. The owner accepts the ADR. Builds on ADR 0102 (the placement grid), 0114 (colour roles), 0115 (worked steps, the automatic layout and the notes in qa.json) and 0118 (strict quality gates). Touches no frozen file and no item of section 15 of the architecture. `docs/ENGINE_INTERFACE.md` changes additively (section 10, contract 0.2): one optional scene field; a request that does not use it is read, keyed and built as before.

## Context
A story is only shapes and text: a host (a model or a person) has to place every circle, line and word with numbers. That makes every video look alike, and it makes the common school-video forms (a checklist, a table, a wrong-versus-right pair, a process of steps, a quiz, a bar chart, a definition card, a closing card) expensive and error prone to write: each needs many shapes, coordinates, sizes and cues, and a model that cannot see the picture gets them wrong. ADR 0115 showed the remedy for one form: a scene field that code expands into ordinary shapes before the sanitiser reads them (`steps`). The plan the owner approved (slice 1 of more kinds of video, no model needed to lay out a picture) asks for the same for eight forms.

## Decision
A scene may carry an optional `template`: typed data for one of eight closed kinds. `job/story-templates.ts` (`expandTemplate`) turns it into raw shapes (text, rect, line, arrow, nothing else) inside the 6 by 6 placement grid's area, with every item brought in by a sentence cue, and `sanitizeStory` reads those shapes exactly as it reads any other (the personal-data and secret check, the colour roles, the automatic layout of ADR 0115, the strict gates of ADR 0118 all apply to them unchanged). The expansion is pure and deterministic: no clock, no randomness, no model, no network, no file.

**Fields are typed data, never pictures.** A template has no coordinates, no colour other than the four roles (`term`, `key`, `look-again`, `checked`, and only where the table below says a role may be given), no free-form shapes and no font. Every text and count is bounded (the limits are in `TEMPLATE_LIMITS`). A field the template does not know is ignored with a note. Anything invalid is dropped with a `story_note` (the dropped list of ADR 0115, which `qa.json` carries as advisory entries) and never fails the job: an invalid item is dropped; a template that cannot be drawn whole (a table with a bad cell, fewer than three steps) is dropped whole, because half a table or a missing step is a wrong picture. Text is stored as given (Devanagari too); the page's fonts decide the glyphs.

**Cues.** Each item has an optional `s`, the sentence of the scene (from 0) that brings it in, as a whole number; left out, the item's own place in reading order (so the title shares sentence 0 with the first item), kept inside the scene's sentences. An item that has a mark that follows it (a tick, a "correct" label, a bar's value) comes in a moment after it by a delay on the same sentence.

**The eight kinds** (all sizes are at least 3.0, so strict QA's text-size rule holds on both page styles; all drawn text is ink unless a role is named; the line, rectangle and arrow colour is ink or the role colour):

| kind | fields | limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `checklist` | `title?`, `items: [{ text, state?, s? }]`, `state` is `done`, `todo` (default) or `wrong` | 6 items, 40 characters; `done` draws a green tick (checked), `wrong` a cross and the text in look-again (so the state is shown by shape and by the "!" mark, not by colour alone) |
| `table` | `title?`, `header: [text]`, `rows: [[cell]] or [{ cells, s? }]`, `highlight?: { row?, col?, s? }` | 2 to 5 columns, 6 rows, a cell is text or a number of at most 24 characters (fewer when the columns are narrow: 14 for 3 columns, 9 for 5); a highlight is a tinted band in the key role |
| `comparison` | `title?`, `left` and `right`: `{ label, role?, s?, points: [{ text, s? }] }` | label 14 characters, 4 points of 20 characters each; `role` lets a panel read as wrong (`look-again`) or right (`checked`) |
| `steps` | `title?`, `steps: [{ text, s? }]` | 3 to 6 numbered boxes joined by arrows, 22 characters each (two lines of 14); more than three run in rows that snake, so every arrow is short |
| `quiz` | `question`, `options: [{ text, s? }]`, `answer?` (a number from 0 or A to D), `reveal?` (a sentence) | question 90 characters (two lines), 2 to 4 options of 34 characters; at `reveal` (default the last sentence) the right option is tinted in the checked role and labelled "correct"; no valid `answer` means nothing is revealed |
| `bars` | `title?`, `bars: [{ label, value, role?, s? }]` | 2 to 8 bars, value 0 to 99,999; the scale ends at the next 1, 2, 2.5, 5 or 10 times a power of ten (`niceMax`), the top and zero are printed, every bar prints its value (at most two decimals); each bar rises by the flood motion of ADR 0096 |
| `definition` | `term`, `meaning`, `example?` | term 24, meaning 120 (three lines), example 70 (two lines, printed after "Example:") |
| `endcard` | `title`, `lines?`, `provenance?` | title 28, 3 lines of 42; the provenance line "Voice and pictures made by a computer program; not checked by a person" is drawn only when `provenance` is `true`, and never otherwise |

**Counts and the shape cap.** A scene keeps at most 28 shapes (ADR 0091). A template's shapes are added after those 28 are counted, so a template is never cut in half (a table is up to 50 shapes). The cap on the shapes the host wrote is unchanged.

**Keys and bytes.** A story with no `template` is read, sanitised, placed and drawn to the same bytes as before: the guard tests hash the sanitised story, the build key and the options of existing story fixtures from before the change. A story with a `template` has a different build key because its request holds more text, which is correct. `template` is not part of any option and no output file changes shape.

**Not decided here.** Which wording the pictures use in Hindi and Nepali (the fonts decide; see the risks); a template for formulas or graphs (steps and plots exist); a second slice of kinds (a number line of marks, a timeline with dates, a labelled diagram).

## Consequences
- Tests (`story-templates.test.ts`): one good scene per kind that expands to the shapes expected and passes the strict gates; a rejecting test for every rule (a count over the limit, a text over its length, a field of the wrong type, an unknown kind, an unknown field, a bad `s`, a bad role, a coordinate or a colour in the data); one corrupted template per kind that must be dropped or noted; the cues (default, given, clamped); the personal-data and secret check reaching template text; determinism; Devanagari text kept as given; the guard hashes of stories with no template. The fixtures `fixtures/templates/*.request.json` (one per kind, `options.qa: "strict"`) are checked to pass every strict gate.
- Wiring: one line in `sanitizeStory` (the shapes of `expandTemplate` follow the capped ones). `story-video.ts`, the renderer and the encoder are not touched.
- Risks: the width of text is an estimate (ADR 0115), so a limit that is a character count is tuned for the widest case of Latin text and is conservative for Devanagari (whose marks count as characters in the estimate but are narrow); a long Devanagari conjunct may still be wider than its estimate. Hindi and Nepali pages ask for Noto Sans Devanagari from Google Fonts (ADR 0115 lists this link as a risk on a machine with no network); on such a machine Devanagari text falls back to whatever font the browser holds, and it was not rendered in this session. A bar chart's value labels are limited by the number of bars (five characters at eight bars), so a long number is dropped with a note rather than printed over its neighbour. A template is laid out for one scene at 100 by 45 units: a host that also draws shapes in the same scene owns what overlaps (strict QA reports it).
- Not built: pixel checks of the rendered picture, a template for a graph, per-item colours beyond the four roles, a new font.
